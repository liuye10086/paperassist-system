# Thesis writing

Follow the supplied chapter purposes, order, and languages and each chapter's frozen material scope. Organize each paragraph around a specific claim and its supporting fact or evidence. Describe gaps in terms of what the selected materials document. Place the full account of a limitation in the section it affects. Elsewhere, state its consequence for that section. Leave review-status notices to the existing review flow.

- **Introduction and literature review:** Compare selected studies on the method, study material, or outcome that matters to the thesis. When introducing a study's finding, keep its selected sample unit and reported sample count with that finding if the evidence supplies them. If a selected abstract documents a protocol relevant to the question, state what it documents and identify any absence of outcome data. Attach the supplied evidence alias and a matching citation anchor to that sentence. Identify what the comparison leaves open and use it to state the question this thesis addresses. When literature is absent, start from the supplied problem and define the scope of the question.
- **Methods:** Describe the study units, measurements, and comparison actually documented. Identify plans as plans. If steps needed to reconstruct the analysis are missing, name the missing details that matter to reproducibility.
- **Results:** Lead with the study's own recorded observation or reported summary. Give the supported magnitude, direction, units, and uncertainty. For a saved illustration, describe its documented role and identify any missing link to measured data. Describe evidence gaps within the scope of the selected materials.
- **Discussion:** In the same passage, connect a specific result to a relevant cited finding, explain the match or difference, and show how the materials, methods, or outcomes limit the comparison. Distinguish the study's evidence and uncertainty from those of earlier studies. With sparse evidence, explain which interpretation is limited by the missing record.
- **Conclusion:** Answer the thesis question within the scope of the supported findings. Where a gap remains, name the evidence most directly needed for the next inference.
- **Abstracts:** Use the completed body to write both abstracts as a pair. Check that both convey the same question, design, main observation, direction, units, quantities, and limits as the results and conclusion. Keep technical terms consistent and use natural phrasing in each language. Use the same frozen number forms in both abstracts when citing bound values, and keep planned quantities distinct from observations.

Use the supplied title for the requested English translation and describe only the requested chapter and figure headings. Return presentation and citation suggestions in the specified fields. Keep manually supplied school content, identity fields, source documents, and reference assembly under local control.
