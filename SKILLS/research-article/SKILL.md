# Research article

**Title and abstract.** State the main finding or study scope in a concise title using terms a reader in the field can recognize. In the abstract, connect the study question, documented design, principal measured finding and supported conclusion. Carry the same observation level, uncertainty and scope into both. Keep decisive planned and reported counts and their attribution. Distinguish a known discrepancy from its cause when the cause is unrecorded.

**Introduction.** Use the background necessary to understand the study question and interpret its results. When selected literature establishes what is already known, use it to identify a specific unresolved question. End the introduction with the study objective and its scope. Briefly state what the study established when the record supports it.

**Methods.** Explain the documented design, procedures and their sequence. Use the observation unit when the record defines it. A count of planned or retained measurements alone does not define the observation unit or the number of independent replicates. Attribute plans to the protocol and completed procedures to the execution record. If a missing detail prevents reproducing a step, identify that detail and the step it affects.

**Results.** Report the primary result first. Within the supplied outline, lead each results subsection with its specific finding. Use consistent wording for groups, outcomes and panels across the text and supplied figures. Name the actual actor or recorded source in ordinary academic prose. Attribute an action such as retaining observations to the person or procedure that performed it, or state that the record describes the retained observations when the actor is unspecified. Give the observed contrast, exact values, available uncertainty and the relevant table or figure. Describe what an illustration shows separately from measurements. Explain retention or exclusion only when the source records the reason.

**Discussion.** Lead with the interpretation of the named result at the level it was measured. Explain how the finding addresses the study question and what it adds to the evidence. State which inference each important limitation constrains and why. Name the evidence needed to resolve an important gap.

When selected literature offers a comparison, set the present result alongside a specific finding with its source and original metric, unit and population or material scope. Compare direction or magnitude only to the extent the contrasts and measurement definitions align. Explain how a documented difference in the observation units, materials or workflow limits the comparison. Keep the study's values and the literature's values bound to their own sources. A descriptive resemblance does not by itself establish equivalence, replication or accuracy.
