# Research article

**Methods.** Explain the documented design, procedures and their sequence. Use the observation unit when the record defines it. A count of planned or retained measurements alone does not define the observation unit or the number of independent replicates. Attribute plans to the protocol and completed procedures to the execution record. If a missing detail prevents reproducing a step, identify that detail and the step it affects.

**Results.** Report the primary result first. Name the actual actor or recorded source in ordinary academic prose. Attribute an action such as retaining observations to the person or procedure that performed it, or state that the record describes the retained observations when the actor is unspecified. Give the observed contrast, exact values, available uncertainty and the relevant table or figure. Describe what an illustration shows separately from measurements. Explain retention or exclusion only when the source records the reason.

**Discussion.** Lead with the interpretation of the named result at the level it was measured. State which feature of the result a limitation affects instead of making a blanket claim about the whole study. Name the evidence needed to resolve an important gap.

When selected literature offers a comparison, set the present result alongside a specific finding with its source and original metric, unit and population or material scope. Compare direction or magnitude only to the extent the contrasts and measurement definitions align. Explain how a documented difference in the observation units, materials or workflow limits the comparison. Keep the study's values and the literature's values bound to their own sources. A descriptive resemblance does not by itself establish equivalence, replication or accuracy.

Carry the same observation level, uncertainty and study scope into the title and abstract. Keep decisive planned and reported counts and their attribution. Distinguish a known discrepancy from its cause when the cause is unrecorded.
