# Narrative review

Open the abstract with the main comparison in a declarative sentence. Use the introduction to explain why that comparison matters and lead into the themes. Give these openings different work instead of repeating a question or restating the abstract. Choose a title whose population and design qualifiers are supported across the studies. Keep study-specific qualifiers with the relevant source. Describe the material read and any documented selection procedure in the literature-scope section.

Organize the evidence around the confirmed themes. Within a theme, present each study's decisive quantitative detail in a paragraph attributed to that study, with its subject, method, scope and reported observation or aim. Use its evidence alias with each detail that changes the comparison, such as a stated coverage count, time point or unit. Use the exact numeric binding when a source writes the count as a word and the catalog provides a corresponding `/quote/cardinals/` value. Close the theme with a paragraph that draws the comparison across those findings.

Explain what the approaches contribute to the shared question and what can be compared from the excerpts. Distinguish differences in study coverage, modality or purpose from differences in observed findings. Connect the most important missing detail to the conclusion it limits. Let the discussion develop the consequence for interpretation, and let the conclusion state the supported inference without retelling the comparison.
