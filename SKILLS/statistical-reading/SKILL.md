name: statistical-reading
description: Explain a fixed statistical figure using its verified facts, method and evidence references.
metadata:
  category: analysis
  version: "1.2.1"
---

Give each part of the six-section explanation its own purpose. Use the surrounding prose to explain how the cited facts fit together. In the first five sections the placeholders carry labels and values, so use a complete clause and avoid repeating the labels.

- **purpose** State the comparison or descriptive question the figure answers. Use the frozen scope to identify the plotted variables, groups or model terms and say what the plot represents.
- **data** Explain what the retained records and plotted marks represent. When projection and original analysis counts are supplied, name the population each describes and explain how the applicable fields or inclusion rules define it. Use the recorded design status when discussing the unit of observation.
- **methods** Explain how the saved procedure produced the series, comparison or estimate. Focus on the applicable order, grouping, reference, denominator and handling of missing records. Tie any supplied assumption or design declaration to the procedure it qualifies.
- **results** Lead with the plotted pattern supported by the saved coordinates or summaries, or with the meaning of the saved estimate for the named comparison. In descriptive plots, relate direction, separation or spread to the plotted records. For statistical estimates, explain the quantity being estimated and report its direction and magnitude on the saved scale with its supplied uncertainty. Use the recorded status or reason when uncertainty is unavailable. Refer to the matching series, summary or parameter fact at the point where it supports the finding.
- **interpretation** Explain which scientific conclusions the result can support and which specific limitations affect them. Where applicable, connect different analysis populations, missing records, design status or diagnostics to the consequence for interpretation. Combine related caveats and keep each applicable limitation bound to its reference.
- **paper_text** Write a short paragraph for a manuscript. State the scientific finding in direct prose using a precise verb for the observed change or comparison. Place the bound estimate, its recorded scale and relevant supplied uncertainty beside the finding. Provide the comparison, method and record population needed to understand it. For a descriptive plot, state the plotted pattern and its boundary for inference. Put the main scientific caveat in a separate sentence using its limitation reference. Omit coordinate inventories, repeated limitations, precision rules and verification notes from this paragraph. Use the other sections for those details.

Use sentence structure that fits the specific result. Avoid lead-ins such as “the finding's stability and coverage are qualified by” and “the inferential boundary is explicit.” For the English line-plot scope, the variable reference reads as the vertical field by the horizontal field. The record references already include the unit of observation. Keep literal count labels, variable names, numeric values and statistical terms inside the appropriate placeholders.

For a logistic forest plot, use the saved coding and reference-line facts when supplied. Explain the intercept as the model baseline, and describe how the plotted parameter's interval bears on uncertainty. Keep quantities such as the number of populations inside their references. Introduce each limitation with a complete sentence that explains its relevance. Let the bound facts carry wording about causality, independence and verification.
